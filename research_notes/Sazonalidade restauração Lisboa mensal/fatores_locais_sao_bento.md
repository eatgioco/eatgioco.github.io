## 1. Public transport ridership as a proxy for residents/workers present (Metro de Lisboa, Carris, CP, Fertagus, Transtejo)

### Takeaway
A full monthly series was not retrievable, but the fragments agree: the busiest Metro months are March and October (≈15 M passengers each in 2023), September is already near peak (14.8 M in 2024), and August is officially treated by the operator as a seasonal low (reduced service in August 2026). The best quantified comparable is Madrid, where August metro ridership is about two-thirds of an average month.

### Cited Findings
- 2023: March was the month with highest Metro de Lisboa demand, 15.194 million passengers, followed by October with 14.923 million (search summary of the article). — [ECO, 14 Sep 2023 / related coverage](https://eco.sapo.pt/2023/09/14/passageiros-no-metro-de-lisboa-ainda-estao-5-abaixo-do-nivel-anterior-a-pandemia/)
- Jan–Aug 2023: Metro carried 104 million passengers vs 111 million in Jan–Aug 2019 (≈5% below pre-pandemic), and 22 million more than Jan–Aug 2022. — [ECO](https://eco.sapo.pt/2023/09/14/passageiros-no-metro-de-lisboa-ainda-estao-5-abaixo-do-nivel-anterior-a-pandemia/); [Jornal de Negócios](https://www.jornaldenegocios.pt/empresas/transportes/detalhe/passageiros-nos-transportes-publicos-aumentam-27-ate-agosto)
- September 2024: 14.8 million passengers with valid ticket, +7.0% vs September 2023. — [Metropolitano de Lisboa press release, 30 Nov 2024](https://www.metrolisboa.pt/institucional/2024/11/30/metropolitano-de-lisboa-atinge-numero-recorde-de-transporte-de-passageiros/); [ECO](https://eco.sapo.pt/2024/11/30/metropolitano-de-lisboa-bate-recorde-de-passageiros-transportados/)

- 2020 (distorted): Metro demand June–August 2020 was 59% below the same period of 2019. — [SOL](https://sol.sapo.pt/artigo/708840/metro-de-lisboa-procura-com-quebra-de-59-entre-junho-e-agosto)

- Madrid, August 2026: about 42.02 million metro passengers (+2.4% y/y). — [Telemadrid, Oct 2026](https://www.telemadrid.es/noticias/madrid/Mas-usuarios-en-Metro-de-Madrid-los-viajeros-del-suburbano-aumentaron-hasta-un-24-en-el-mes-de-agosto-0-2933106663--20261009095555.html)

### Inferences
- **Lisbon, partial arithmetic (2023):** Jan–Aug mean = 104 ÷ 8 = 13.0 M/month, vs October 14.923 M → the average of the first eight months is 12.9% below October. Since March alone was 15.194 M, the other seven months (Jan, Feb, Apr–Aug) average (104 − 15.194) ÷ 7 = 12.69 M, i.e. 15% below October. August must be below that average for the operator to call it a seasonal low, so **August is more than ~15% below October**, but the exact figure is not established here.
- **Madrid index (computed):** 2024 monthly mean = 715.2 ÷ 12 = 59.6 M; August 39.6 M → index 39.6 ÷ 59.6 × 100 = **66** (August ≈ 34% below an average month). Caveat: the August figure is INE-Spain as reported by Telemadrid and the annual figure is the regional consortium's, so the two may not be strictly comparable. This is a reasonable order of magnitude for a Southern European capital's "commuter presence" in August; Lisbon's Metro has a larger tourist share, so its August dip is probably shallower.
- **Internal inconsistency to flag:** 2024 = 176.727 M and +6.5% implies 2023 ≈ 165.9 M; with Jan–Aug = 104 M that leaves ≈ 61.9 M for Sep–Dec, i.e. 15.5 M/month, which is higher than the stated 2023 peak month (15.194 M). The "104 M" and the monthly figures therefore probably use different definitions (e.g. valid-ticket validations vs. total estimated passengers) or rounding. Do not chain these numbers into an index.
- Shape supported by the fragments: peaks in March and October(–November), September already ≈ peak level, trough in August, January roughly at normal level (Jan 2024 above Jan 2019).

### Gaps
- **Complete monthly Metro de Lisboa series (2018–2019, 2022–2026) not obtained.** It exists in: Metropolitano de Lisboa quarterly "PAO" execution reports (e.g. [Q3 2024](https://www.metrolisboa.pt/institucional/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2025/04/PAO_3ro_trim_2024.pdf), [Q2 2024](https://www.metrolisboa.pt/institucional/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2025/04/PAO_2do_trim_2024.pdf), [Q1 2024](https://www.metrolisboa.pt/institucional/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2025/04/PAO_1ro_trim_2024.pdf)), the AMT metro systems report [2018–2020](https://www.amt-autoridade.pt/media/3324/relatorio_dos_sistemas_de_metro_2018-2020.pdf) and the AMT [2025 performance report](https://www.amt-autoridade.pt/media/5945/2025_relatorio_desempenho_mlepe.pdf), and INE's monthly "Atividade dos transportes". - August value for any year not found; the August-vs-October/November percentage for Lisbon is therefore not established.

## 4. Assembleia da República calendar and headcount

### Takeaway
Parliament sits in plenary from mid-September to mid/late July; from about 18 July to mid-September only the Comissão Permanente meets. That gives roughly eight weeks with no plenary, covering all of August. Budget season (October–November) is the most intense period. Headcount on site could not be sourced beyond the 230 deputies.

### Cited Findings
- 2026: last plenary before the summer break was Friday 17 July 2026; deputies return in September for the Comissão Permanente or the next plenary. — [Observador, 17 Jul 2026](https://observador.pt/2026/07/17/aguiar-branco-despede-se-com-humor-antes-das-ferias-parlamentares-apos-duas-horas-de-votacoes/)

- During the break the Comissão Permanente keeps working and can call extraordinary plenaries (happened in August 2025). — [DN](https://www.dn.pt/política/comisso-permanente-da-assembleia-da-repblica-quase-a-mesma-do-vero-passado)

### Inferences
- Months with full plenary activity: October–June plus the first half of July and second half of September. No plenary: ~18 July to ~mid-September. Shorter pauses at Christmas/New Year and Easter are customary but were not dated here.
- 106 plenary meetings over a ~13.5-month session is consistent with roughly 2–3 plenary days per sitting week (plenaries are typically mid-week to Friday), meaning deputy presence in São Bento is concentrated mid-week even in session months.

## 6. Public holidays, "pontes" and working days per month

### Takeaway
Working days (Mon–Fri excluding national holidays and Lisbon's 13 June) range from 20 to 23 per month. June and December are structurally the shortest working months alongside February; July is the longest. Year-specific holiday placement moves a month by ±1–2 days (±5–10%).

### Inferences
- Likely "ponte" days (weekday between a holiday and a weekend) in 2026: Fri 5 Jun (after Corpo de Deus), Mon 30 Nov and Mon 7 Dec (before the two Tuesday holidays). ## 7. Weather climatology and weather sensitivity of sales/footfall

### Takeaway
Lisbon has a sharply seasonal rain regime: about 10 rainy days a month from October to January (104–134 mm) versus ~1 in July–August. Evidence on sales impact is directional rather than a clean elasticity: rain cuts UK high-street footfall by a few percent in wet weeks and shifts demand to delivery; one US study found temperature mattered more than rain and lunch was the most weather-sensitive meal.

- US QSR traffic report (2019; NYC, Chicago, Atlanta): QSRs are typically less affected by weather than casual dining; bakeries/frozen-yogurt saw lighter sales in rain, noodle/ramen more. — [QSRweb](https://www.qsrweb.com/news/the-weather-effect-report-tracks-weather-trends-related-to-dining-traffic)
- UK footfall: in one wet week high-street footfall fell 2.2% week-on-week vs 1.3% in shopping centres and 1.0% in retail parks (Springboard); a wet October saw high-street footfall −7.4%; an MRI week showed high streets −4.4%; headline "rain drives high street footfall down by 6%". — [Drapers](https://www.drapersonline.com/news/rainy-weather-dampens-footfall); [Insight DIY / BRC-Springboard](https://www.insightdiy.co.uk/news/brcspringboard-autumn-rain-dampens-retail-footfall/7781.htm); [Retail Times / MRI](https://retailtimes.co.uk/mri-software-high-street-footfall-slips-by-7-9-as-retail-hit-by-rainy-weather/); [Evening Standard via Magzter](https://www.magzter.com/stories/newspaper/Evening-Standard/RAIN-DRIVES-HIGH-STREET-FOOTFALL-DOWN-BY-6)
- UK snow event 2018: restaurants on Tenzo's system −27% year-on-year. — [Tenzo](https://www.gotenzo.com/resources/insight/brace-yourself-restaurateurs-winter-is-coming-the-huge-effects-of-snow-on-restaurants-pubs-and-cafes/)
- Delivery: a study of ~4.3 million orders in three Chinese cities found online food orders rise with rainfall and temperature; a Domino's manager reported delivery orders up almost 40% in rain (anecdotal); Bite Squad deliveries +12% on a cold January Monday. — [Theoretical and Applied Climatology 2023](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ThApC.153.1425Y/abstract); [Dallas Observer](https://www.dallasobserver.com/food-drink/weather-or-not-6418937/); [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/delivery-throws-restaurants-a-lifeline-during-extreme-weather/549128/)
- Unverified vendor claim: a fully rainy day cuts street-level footfall by up to 7.5% (no primary source cited). — [Silent Infotech blog](https://silentinfotech.com/blog/integration-6/five-weather-patterns-that-are-costing-your-retail-store-460)

### Inferences
- Rain-day share of the month (rainy days ÷ days in month): Jan 32%, Feb 28%, Mar 25%, Apr 26%, May 19%, Jun 7%, Jul 2%, Aug 4%, Sep 13%, Oct 28%, Nov 33%, Dec 32%. If a rainy day costs a street-facing counter something like the 2–7% footfall drop seen on UK high streets, the monthly drag would be at most ~1–2% in Nov–Jan and essentially nil in Jun–Aug — i.e. rain is a day-level risk, small at monthly level. This transfers UK retail evidence to Lisbon food and is an assumption, not a measurement.
- The UK numbers are week-on-week/year-on-year changes confounded by other factors, not isolated rain effects.

### Gaps
- IPMA monthly mean-max temperatures for all 12 months, days ≥30 °C, and 1991–2020 sunshine hours — not retrieved from IPMA directly.
- No Portuguese or Southern European POS/footfall study quantifying rain or heat effects on takeaway/lunch sales was found; no per-degree elasticity.
- No Uber Eats/Glovo seasonality data for Portugal found.

## 10. Characteristics of the São Bento / Príncipe Real / Estrela / Santos area

### Takeaway
The shop sits on the boundary of two small, shrinking resident parishes — Estrela (20,267 residents) and Misericórdia (9,658, down 26% since 2011) — the latter with one of Lisbon's highest short-term-rental ratios, so the resident base is thin and partly replaced by visitors.

### Cited Findings
- Estrela parish: 20,267 residents (Censos 2021), 4.60 km², 4,405.9 inhabitants/km²; formed in 2012 from Lapa, Prazeres and Santos-o-Velho. — [Wikipedia PT, Estrela (Lisboa)](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estrela_(Lisboa)); [aldeiasportuguesas.pt](https://aldeiasportuguesas.pt/lisboa/lisboa/estrela/)
- Misericórdia parish: 9,658 residents in 2021 (4,809 men, 4,849 women), 2.19 km²; 13,044 in 2011 (≈−26%); smallest Lisbon parish by population; formed from Encarnação, Santa Catarina, Mercês and São Paulo. — [Wikipedia PT, Misericórdia (Lisboa)](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miseric%C3%B3rdia_(Lisboa)); [Observatório de Luta contra a Pobreza na Cidade de Lisboa](https://observatorio-lisboa.eapn.pt/freguesias/misericordia/)
- Minor discrepancies: other sites give 9,660 or 9,645 for Misericórdia and 20,308 for Estrela. — [aldeiasportuguesas.pt](https://aldeiasportuguesas.pt/lisboa/lisboa/)
- Short-term rental (AL) ratio after Censos 2021 revision: Santa Maria Maior rose from 52% to 60.7% and Misericórdia from 39% to 40.8% (ratio definition not shown in the excerpt; believed to be AL units relative to dwellings). — [Idealista News](https://www.idealista.pt/news/node/56486)
- Nearly 20% of Lisbon's population is foreign (DN headline). — [DN](https://www.dn.pt/politica/quase-20-da-populacao-de-lisboa-e-estrangeira-mas-so-28-podem-votar--13994679.html)

### Inferences
- Combined resident population of the two parishes ≈ 29,925 (20,267 + 9,658) over 6.79 km². With ~41% AL ratio in Misericórdia, part of the "resident" housing stock behaves like tourist demand (peaks in summer), which partly offsets the August loss of residents and workers — the direction of the net effect for this address is not measurable from these sources.

### Gaps
- Offices/embassies count, share of foreign residents by parish, tram 28 passenger flows, Rua de São Bento commercial description (antique shops) — nothing citable retrieved.
- Príncipe Real's parish placement was not confirmed by the sources found.
